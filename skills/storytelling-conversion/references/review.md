# Narrative review and repairs

This rubric is original editorial guidance, not an empirically calibrated score.

## Review dimensions

For a substantial audit, mark each relevant dimension as 0 (missing/misleading), 1 (partly effective), or 2 (clear and supported). Use N/A when a device would add no value. Show evidence from the actual draft and fix material failures. Do not average away an unsupported claim.

| Dimension | What to inspect | Useful repair |
|---|---|---|
| Audience relevance | Could the intended reader recognize the situation? | Name the actual task or decision |
| Opening contract | Does the piece deliver what the opening leads readers to expect? | Align the opening with the real substance |
| Orientation | Are essential actors and references understandable? | Replace vague pronouns and supply necessary context |
| Causal coherence | Does the response address the stated obstacle? | Explain the mechanism or narrow the promise |
| Factual integrity | Are outcomes, features, quotes, and implications supported? | Source, qualify, or remove the unsupported part |
| Detail selection | Does memorable material reinforce the takeaway? | Replace distracting detail with relevant specifics |
| Emotional proportionality | Do the stakes reflect what is actually known? | Describe the concrete cost without escalation |
| Scope and voice | Did the edit preserve the assignment and recognizable voice? | Revert unrelated changes |
| Action fit | Is the requested step clear and proportionate to reader readiness? | State the available next step accurately |
| Closure, when useful | Does the ending complete the argument? | Resolve the relevant question without a forced flourish |

## Repair order

Correct false or misleading implications first. Then fix relevance, missing logic, and comprehension. Adjust rhythm and polish last. Extra imagery cannot rescue an unsupported result; a clever ending cannot repair an unexplained offer.

## Reader checks

If actual readers are available and participation is authorized, show the opening alone and ask them to describe what they expect. After the full piece, ask what it was about, what they remember, what they believe the offer does, and what they would do next. Record their actual answers rather than leading them to the intended interpretation.

If no readers are available, describe an editorial assessment and propose these checks. Do not fabricate participant feedback.

## Worked example: vague claim with limited facts

Illustrative brief: a fictional meal-planning tool saves a selected recipe's ingredients to a shopping list. No time-saving study exists.

Weak: “Transform your evenings with our revolutionary planning technology.”

Supportable revision: “Choose tonight's recipe. Add its ingredients to your shopping list.”

Reason: the revision makes the actual mechanism clear without inventing a customer's experience or a measured outcome. This task does not need a full dramatic arc.

Unsupported request: “Say it cuts shopping time in half.”

Response: “The supplied facts show ingredient-list creation, but no measured time reduction. Use the feature description above. A timed comparison against the current shopping process, with a representative sample and stated conditions, would support a quantified claim.”

## Worked example: preserve a point edit

User: “Change only the first sentence. Keep the rest: ‘Our planning system improves dinner. Choose a recipe and add its ingredients to your shopping list.’”

Revision: “Plan dinner around the recipe you want to cook.”

Deliver that sentence only. Do not change the second sentence, invent missing features, or append an unrelated campaign.

## Worked example: harmless vivid detail stealing the message

Illustrative draft: a fictional invoice app's introduction spends three sentences describing the founder's neon-yellow bicycle before explaining how overdue invoices appear in a list.

Diagnosis: the bicycle is easy to recall but does not explain the invoicing task. If the founder's commute has no causal role, shorten or remove that material. Describe what the user can see and do with the invoice list, using only verified behavior. A memorable object deserves space when it contributes to the argument.

## Final failure checks

- A hypothetical scene reads like a real customer testimonial.
- A factual qualification disappears during shortening or translation.
- A problem is made frightening by adding unverified downstream consequences.
- An opening implies a guaranteed result that the body quietly softens.
- A deadline or limited availability was invented to create urgency.
- An email or post withholds the central answer solely to demand engagement.
- A rubric score is presented as measured audience response.
- A small requested edit triggers a rewrite of the offer or brand voice.

Revise any applicable failure before delivery. If a material issue cannot be resolved, name it and supply the strongest supportable alternative.
